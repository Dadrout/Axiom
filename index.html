Forces & motion

First law: inertia

An object stays at rest or keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed unless a net external force acts on it.

ΣF = 0 → a = 0

ExampleA book resting on a table stays still: the table's upward force balances gravity.

Second law: acceleration

For an object with constant mass, acceleration depends on the net force. More force means more acceleration; more mass means less acceleration for the same force.

ΣF = ma

ExampleA net force of 10 N acting on a 2 kg object gives it an acceleration of 5 m/s².

Third law: action & reaction

When one object exerts a force on another, the second exerts an equal force in the opposite direction on the first. These forces act on different objects.

FAB = −FBA

ExampleWhen you jump, you push down on the ground and the ground pushes up on you.

Energy & work

Kinetic energy

Moving objects carry kinetic energy. For the same mass, doubling the speed gives four times the kinetic energy.

Eₖ = ½mv²

ExampleA 2 kg ball moving at 3 m/s has 9 J of kinetic energy.

Gravitational potential energy

Near Earth’s surface, raising an object stores energy. Here, m is mass, g is gravitational acceleration, and h is height above a chosen reference.

Eₚ = mgh

ExampleLifting a 1 kg book by 2 m stores about 19.6 J of energy.

Work done by a force

A constant force transfers energy when an object moves. Only the component of force along the displacement does work.

W = Fd cos θ

ExamplePushing a box 3 m with a 10 N force along its motion transfers 30 J.

Waves & sound

Wave speed

A wave carries a disturbance. Its speed equals its frequency multiplied by its wavelength.

v = fλ

ExampleA 2 Hz wave with a wavelength of 3 m travels at 6 m/s.

Frequency & period

Frequency counts cycles per second. The period is the time for one cycle; higher frequency means a shorter period.

T = 1/f

ExampleA 5 Hz wave completes one cycle every 0.2 seconds.

Sound needs a medium

Sound travels through vibrations in matter, such as air, water, or metal. It cannot travel through a vacuum.

v ≈ 343 m/s

ExampleIn air at about 20°C, sound travels roughly 343 m in one second.

Electricity & circuits

Ohm’s law

For an ohmic component under constant physical conditions, voltage equals current multiplied by resistance.

V = IR

ExampleA current of 2 A through a 3 Ω resistor requires 6 V.

Electrical power

Power tells us how quickly electrical energy is transferred. Multiply voltage by current to find the power.

P = VI

ExampleA device drawing 2 A from a 12 V supply uses 24 W.

Electric charge

For a constant current, the charge passing a point equals the current multiplied by the elapsed time.

Q = It

ExampleA current of 3 A flowing for 4 seconds transfers 12 C of charge.

Heat & temperature

Heating a material

When no phase change occurs, the heat needed depends on mass, specific heat capacity, and temperature change.

Q = mcΔT

ExampleHeating 1 kg of water by 1°C takes about 4,180 J.

First law of thermodynamics

Energy is conserved. A system’s internal energy increases when heat enters it and decreases when it does work on its surroundings.

ΔU = Q − W

ExampleIf a gas receives 100 J of heat and does 30 J of work, its internal energy rises by 70 J.

Ideal gas law

For an ideal gas, pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas are related. Temperature must be measured in kelvin.

pV = nRT

ExampleIn a sealed rigid container, doubling an ideal gas’s temperature in kelvin doubles its pressure.

The people behind the ideas.

Meet six thinkers whose discoveries changed how we understand the physical world.

Isaac Newton

1643–1727

Motion & gravity

Formulated three laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation, connecting everyday motion with the motion of planets.

Michael Faraday

1791–1867

Electromagnetic induction

Discovered that a changing magnetic field can induce an electric current—the principle behind electric generators.

James Clerk Maxwell

1831–1879

Electricity, magnetism & light

United electricity and magnetism in a mathematical theory and showed that light is an electromagnetic wave.

Marie Curie

1867–1934

Radioactivity

Pioneered research into radioactivity and, with Pierre Curie, discovered polonium and radium. She received Nobel Prizes in both physics and chemistry.

Albert Einstein

1879–1955

Relativity & quantum physics

Developed special and general relativity. His explanation of the photoelectric effect helped establish the quantum nature of light.

Emmy Noether

1882–1935

Symmetry & conservation

Showed how continuous symmetries of a physical system lead to conservation laws. Time-translation symmetry, for example, is linked to conservation of energy.

A spring in motion.

A spring stores energy when you stretch it. Let go, and that energy becomes motion as the mass moves back toward equilibrium.

F = −kx

Hooke’s law describes an ideal spring within its elastic range. The restoring force is proportional to displacement and points in the opposite direction. With no friction or air resistance, the mass keeps oscillating.
